## [Webhooks vs. SCROLL: Rethinking State Sync](https://weli.dev/blog/the-valley-of-webhooks/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The blog post critiques webhook-based state synchronization and proposes SCROLL, a pseudo‑IETF subscription protocol that mirrors an existing IETF draft for HTTP subscriptions. Reliable state sync is critical for distributed systems; the post highlights webhook pitfalls and offers a protocol that could standardize subscription mechanisms, potentially improving consistency and reducing latency. SCROLL uses a GET request with a Prefer: stream header, establishing a persistent connection similar to HTTP push, but the author notes that consumers will maintain a connection regardless of event frequency, raising efficiency concerns.

hackernews · weli · Aug 5, 15:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49184216)

**Background**: Webhooks are event callbacks that push data to clients, but they can be unreliable, hard to deduplicate, and may require polling for consistency. Subscription protocols, such as those defined in IETF drafts, aim to provide a standardized, pull‑based or push‑based mechanism for clients to receive updates over HTTP. The SCROLL protocol described in the blog mirrors a draft that uses GET with a stream preference to maintain a continuous data flow.
