## [AI-Generated Phages: First Viable Genomes from Genome Language Models](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vjj4pr/r_generative_design_of_novel_bacteriophages_with/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers used the Evo 1 and Evo 2 genome language models to generate 16 novel, viable bacteriophage genomes based on the ΦX174 lytic phage template, marking the first successful creation of functional whole-genome phage sequences by AI. This breakthrough demonstrates that AI can design entire viral genomes with realistic genetic architecture and host specificity, opening new avenues for rapid, custom phage therapy development and advancing synthetic biology. The Evo models operate at single‑nucleotide resolution with up to 1 million base‑pair context, enabling realistic gene arrangement and regulatory elements; however, the study focused on a single lytic ssDNA phage, and further validation is needed to assess safety and efficacy in diverse hosts.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/moschles · Aug 9, 07:11

**Background**: Genome language models \(gLMs\) are large language models trained on DNA sequences to predict and generate genetic content. Evo 2, a state‑of‑the‑art gLM, was trained on 9 trillion base pairs across all life domains, providing a 1 million‑token context and 40 billion parameters. The ΦX174 phage is a small, single‑stranded DNA virus \(~5.4 kb\) that infects Escherichia coli and has been a classic model for viral genetics.

A security researcher released an algorithm that generates printable adversarial patches capable of fooling surveillance camera object detectors, effectively hiding people, faces, and vehicles from real‑time detection. This breakthrough undermines the reliability of AI‑driven surveillance, raising privacy concerns and prompting a reevaluation of security protocols in public spaces. The patches are optimized via gradient‑based loss functions against convolutional neural networks and can be printed on clothing or applied to objects; however, they may lose effectiveness under extreme lighting or camera angles.

rss · TechCrunch · Aug 9, 14:00

**Background**: Adversarial machine learning exploits small perturbations to input data that cause deep learning models to misclassify. In computer vision, adversarial patches are physical patterns that, when placed in a scene, cause convolutional neural networks to miss objects. Surveillance systems increasingly rely on CNN‑based detectors for people, face, and vehicle recognition, making them vulnerable to such attacks.
