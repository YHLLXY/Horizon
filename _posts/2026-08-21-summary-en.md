## [LLMs Save Money When Asked to Be Concise, Study Finds](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vulfei/does_telling_an_llm_to_be_concise_actually_save/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study across nine LLMs shows that prompting models to produce concise outputs reduces token usage and cost by about 1.5× on average, while maintaining accuracy. Shortening input prompts, however, increases cost and hurts accuracy. Cost savings are critical for developers deploying LLMs at scale, and the finding that output brevity is more effective than input trimming informs prompt engineering best practices. It also highlights that providers’ concise output options may not automatically translate to lower costs without careful token control. The study tested Claude Haiku, Claude Sonnet, GPT‑4o, GPT‑5.4, Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B, Qwen3.5‑9B, DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill, Gemma‑4‑E4B, and Kimi‑K2.6 across five short‑answer datasets, an eleven‑language output run, and a summarization task. Output tokens are more expensive than input tokens, so targeting fewer output tokens yields savings in single‑turn tasks.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/ibubbles34 · Aug 21, 16:38

**Background**: Large language models \(LLMs\) generate text token by token, and the cost of using an API is typically proportional to the number of input and output tokens. Prompt engineering techniques such as instructing the model to be concise or trimming the prompt are common ways to control output length and cost. Claude Code, a coding assistant from Anthropic, recently introduced a concise output style that keeps responses short. Models like Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B and DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill are open‑source or open‑foundation models that support multilingual and multimodal tasks.

## [Felony Bench: AI Agent Incident Tracking &amp; Liability](https://www.felonybench.com/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new website, Felony Bench, has been launched to catalog incidents where AI agents unintentionally impact third parties and to analyze the legal liability of users, hosts, and developers under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act \(CFAA\). The platform aggregates unique cases and provides a framework for determining potential criminal responsibility. As AI agents become more autonomous, the line between tool and actor blurs, raising questions about who is culpable for unlawful actions. Clarifying liability helps developers design safer systems, protects users, and informs regulators about enforcement priorities. Felony Bench relies on publicly reported incidents and legal precedent, notably the Ninth Circuit’s recent rulings that treat AI agents as tools rather than independent actors. The site’s name may overstate the severity of incidents, as many cases lack proven intent, but it serves as a useful registry for researchers and policymakers.

**Background**: AI agents are software programs that can autonomously interact with third‑party systems, often using large language models \(LLMs\). The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act \(CFAA\) criminalizes unauthorized access to computer systems, but its application to AI agents has been contested. Recent Ninth Circuit rulings have clarified that AI agents are tools, not independent actors, limiting the scope of CFAA liability.

## [Aaron Swartz vs Meta: Legal Double Standard](https://zeli.app/zh/digest/2026-08-20) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Aaron Swartz, the co‑creator of RSS, was prosecuted for downloading about 70 GB of academic articles from JSTOR, facing a 35‑year sentence and hefty fines. In contrast, Meta reportedly used 80 TB of pirated books to train its AI models, yet faced only symbolic penalties. The case highlights a stark disparity in how the legal system treats individual activists versus large corporations, raising concerns about accountability and the ethics of data harvesting for AI. Swartz’s prosecution was driven by the 2011 Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, while Meta’s data acquisition was largely unregulated under current U.S. copyright law, exposing gaps in enforcement.

rss · Zeli · Aug 20, 23:59

**Background**: Aaron Swartz co‑founded the RSS syndication format, a simple XML‑based web feed that lets users stay updated on content without manual checks. JSTOR is a digital library that archives academic journals and books, providing full‑text access to researchers. Meta’s AI initiatives rely on large datasets to train language models, and the company has publicly disclosed its data‑collection practices through its Transparency Center.
